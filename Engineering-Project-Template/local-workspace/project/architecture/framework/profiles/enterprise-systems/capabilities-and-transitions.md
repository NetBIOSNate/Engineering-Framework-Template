# Profile for enterprise capabilities and federated systems

Use this path when independently owned systems must produce an outcome together. Begin with a capability map describing what the organization needs to do, the beneficiary and the measure of completion. Map applications, people, information, facilities or services onto that capability in a second pass. A product name alone cannot explain what work would stop if it disappeared.

## Define the transition architecture

1. Describe the baseline. Trace one service outcome across organizational boundaries and mark wait states, information authority, duplication and work performed outside managed systems.
2. Describe the target in terms of changed capabilities, owners, information contracts and measurable outcomes. Identify which participants can change on their own schedule and which parties have the authority to bind others.
3. Lay out feasible intermediate states. Give each dependency a delivering and receiving owner, a lead time and a fallback. Show where funding and operational responsibility move.
4. Prioritize investments by the constraints they remove. A shared identifier may unlock several transitions, but its owner must provide reconciliation, lifecycle and dispute resolution.

## Example of distributed authority

Three regional operators share downstream reporting while retaining control over field actions. A common observation identifier and unit vocabulary can support aggregation. A central command channel would change authority and may violate the regions' operating agreements. Choose the smallest shared contract that provides the reporting outcome. Establish a version window and behavior for a region that remains offline during a synchronized policy change.

Review one region leaving the agreement, a new participant joining with different terminology and a period in which two definitions coexist. Verify each participant can recognize covered records, local authority survives the agreed partition and central reports disclose gaps. Produce baseline, interim and target views with named benefit, budget and risk owners.
