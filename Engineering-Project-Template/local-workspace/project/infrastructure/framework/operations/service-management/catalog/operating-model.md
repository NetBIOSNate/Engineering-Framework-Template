# Define the service and the team that can operate it

## Offer a bounded service

Write the request in a consumer's terms. “Provide a private zone for project names with a measured update interval, validated delegation and an emergency rollback contact” makes support and acceptance observable. Identify the service owner, request interface, supported environments, maintenance window and onboarding and exit conditions.

| Activity | Decision right | Evidence to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New service request | Owner accepts a supported class or reviews an exception. | Consumer, quota, operational target, allowed region and funding. |
| Routine change | Designated operator or automation works within a tested change class. | Reviewed input, target, stop condition, result and updated inventory. |
| Incident | Incident leader directs mitigation and service restoration. | Impact timeline, action owners, handoff and consumer validation. |
| Recurring fault | Technical owner investigates causes across incidents. | Measurable recurrence signature and verified corrective change. |
| End of service | Owner coordinates consumers, data custody and asset disposition. | Completed migration, retention decisions and closure evidence. |

## Establish a support boundary

Define staffed hours, what pages immediately, when work can wait and the second responder. Map supplier and facilities escalation into the same record. Check how a request is authenticated, when the consumer receives an acknowledgement and who can authorize a change that affects another project.

## Operate the queue

Sample requests and incidents from a representative month. Measure time waiting for a decision, repeated manual interventions and failure patterns. Automate a frequent action only after defining safe inputs, observable completion and a manual recovery path. Rehearse a handoff to someone outside the build team. The service is supportable when that person can locate authority, evidence and the next decision.
