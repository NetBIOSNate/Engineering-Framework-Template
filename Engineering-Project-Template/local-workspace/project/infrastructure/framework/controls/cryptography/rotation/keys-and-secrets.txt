Keys, secrets and certificate rotation

Goal
Rotate a credential or certificate through its complete dependency chain and recover encrypted infrastructure data after losing the primary environment.

Implementation
1. Inventory each secret by reference, use, issuer, consumer, storage boundary, cryptoperiod or expiry, rotation owner and revocation mechanism. Include agents installed on spare hardware, backup readers and machines disconnected from the network. Keep secret values out of inventories, state previews, tickets and command histories. Identify any decrypted copies a workflow creates.
2. Separate the authority to administer keys from permission to decrypt where supported. Limit each workload's access to its required key and resource. Determine how recovery staff will reach keys, the authorization service and audit records during a site outage. Protect old key versions for as long as retained data needs them. Document the effect of irreversible key destruction.
3. For planned rotation, introduce the new credential or trust chain, update one consumer, confirm a fresh session, roll through remaining consumers and observe old credential use. Revoke the old credential after the agreed overlap and prove the denial. For suspected compromise, have the incident owner choose immediate revocation and a controlled disruption when overlap would prolong exposure.
4. Restore a backup created before rotation into an isolated environment with recovery credentials. Open representative encrypted data and verify integrity. Exercise certificate renewal and validation from a cold start so cached sessions or intermediates cannot hide an error. Include a device with a clock near its allowed offset.

Handoff
Record successful and failed probes, missed consumers, fallback expiry and the named owner for each remaining dependency.

Reference
NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev. 5, Recommendation for Key Management
https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/57/pt1/r5/final
