# Supplier support, concentration and exit

## Goal

Know how a critical capability will operate through supplier disruption, incompatibility or contract end. Apply to managed services, carriers, appliance vendors, licensed recovery tooling and spare-parts providers.

1. Draw both contractual and technical dependencies. Record who owns hardware, patching, configuration, recovery, incident evidence and deletion. Identify dependencies on shared regions, upstream providers, remote licensing, firmware distribution and specific support staff.
2. Request observable outputs for each responsibility. For a managed database, state who can retrieve a usable logical or physical export, whether keys are portable, which historical versions are available and how long export takes. For a carrier, capture the demarcation point, restoration commitment and escalation route.
3. Model exit with a dated sample. Include export bandwidth, transformation, duplicate running cost, transfer charges, replacement lead time, skills and data validation. Two sellers relying on the same last-mile duct or identity service still share a failure.
4. Restore the export into an approved alternate target without privileged supplier assistance where that is part of the exit promise. Test client readback, permissions and integrity. Record unavoidable proprietary dependencies and an owner for the remaining exposure.

## Review trigger

Review before contract renewal, unsupported firmware, major data growth or a change in data-handling scope. Put the last economically viable decision date on the service record. Preserve contacts and entitlements somewhere operators can reach during supplier-portal or primary-site loss.
