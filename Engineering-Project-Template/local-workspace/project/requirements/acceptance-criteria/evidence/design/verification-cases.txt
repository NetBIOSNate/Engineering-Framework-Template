Design evidence before the architecture is fixed

Derive an impartial pass, fail or inconclusive decision from a requirement and a named configuration.

1. Identify the exact claim, boundary, affected population and acceptance authority. Choose inspection for an observable property, analysis for an argued bound, demonstration for an operational capability or test for a controlled comparison. Explain why the method can reveal the failure of interest.
2. Define the setup, stimulus, observable output, expected result and allowable uncertainty. For a maximum allowed travel of 8.0 mm and an agreed bounded measurement error of 0.2 mm, a conservative pass requires the observation plus 0.2 mm to be at most 8.0 mm. If the uncertainty interval crosses the limit, record an inconclusive result and obtain better evidence.
3. Include nominal, threshold, just-outside, degraded and interrupted cases selected for the claim. Identify which requirement requires repetition across variants. Record instrument identity, calibration validity and configuration revision.
4. Assign a reviewer who can challenge the evidence. Keep actual observations, discrepancy IDs and disposition alongside the expected results.

Review output. Another assessor can reconstruct the decision, including its uncertainty, from the evidence package without asking the test designer.
