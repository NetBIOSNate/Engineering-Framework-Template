Bound interference between tenants

Use this profile when multiple users, sites or organizations share a constrained resource. Write the service promise for a named tenant class and workload. The service owner and commercial owner agree whether fairness means equal shares, purchased entitlements or a minimum under contention. Identify any downstream bottleneck that an admission decision cannot see.

1. Define the unit of demand. A ten-second export and a ten-millisecond lookup consume different resources. Model worker-seconds, bytes/s or another measured limiting quantity. Include retries and background jobs in the same accounting boundary.
2. For illustration, a pool can supply 100 worker-seconds each second. Reserving 20 for operations leaves 80. Four tenants with commitments of 15 worker-seconds/s use 60, leaving 20 for bursts. A single first-in, first-out queue can still delay a small tenant behind an accepted burst. Specify queue isolation, deadline and overload response as observable obligations.
3. State how reservations behave when a tenant is idle and how quickly loaned capacity must be reclaimable. Define how clients learn when to retry.

Saturate the pool with one tenant and measure admitted, rejected and delayed work for each of the others. Reconcile per-tenant results with the pool total. Reopen the capacity budget when job mixes, guarantees or dependencies change.
