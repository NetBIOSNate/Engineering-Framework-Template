Keep referred cases within human review capacity

Use when a model can refer uncertain cases to a person. The decision owner defines the adverse consequence of each error. The operations lead owns human review capacity. Name who can suspend model-driven decisions and how already queued cases will be handled. Preserve the model version and decision rule attached to each case.

1. Select evaluation slices that represent actual inputs, including uncommon equipment, languages or acquisition conditions. Define the reference labels, label delay and treatment of ambiguous cases. Include a time-separated evaluation and state how repeated observations from the same subject are handled.
2. Translate model behavior into operational demand. An illustrative service receives 1,200 cases/hour. An 8 percent referral rate generates 96 reviews/hour. Three reviewers completing 25 cases/hour each leave a deficit of 21 cases/hour before breaks and rework. Require a bound on queue age and specify the fallback when staffing or the mix changes.
3. Define the release decision against agreed error costs, referral volume and the worst relevant slice. A small slice with few labels may require further evidence. Record who can accept that uncertainty and for how long.

Replay a shift in case mix and a loss of review capacity. Obtain evidence from arrival to final disposition, including delayed or abandoned cases. Reopen after a model, threshold, input source or staffing change.
