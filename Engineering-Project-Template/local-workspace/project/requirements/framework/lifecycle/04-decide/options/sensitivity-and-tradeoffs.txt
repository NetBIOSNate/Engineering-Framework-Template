Choose among feasible architectures

Establish which candidate satisfies each binding obligation, where the choice is sensitive to uncertain demand, and which party accepts each consequence. Keep a feasible baseline option, including continued operation with a process change when appropriate.

1. Describe candidates at comparable detail against the same workload and operating modes. Apply hard constraints before scoring preferences. A weighted score cannot compensate for an unacceptable failure mode.
2. Estimate ranges and state assumptions. Pick uncertainties that could reverse the ranking. Change one and then several together, including dependencies between cost, load and recovery time. A number derived from expert judgment should identify its source.
3. Name the option, decisive evidence, rejected alternatives and conditions for reopening the decision. Record who accepts the residual risk and the expiry of any temporary concession.

Example. For a planning horizon of one year, option A costs 120,000 plus 2 per unit, and B costs 40,000 plus 6 per unit. Equal cost occurs at 20,000 units. A demand range of 12,000 to 26,000 crosses that point. If both meet mandatory constraints, investigate demand or consider reversible capacity commitments before choosing on cost alone.

Review record. Another planner can reproduce the comparison, find the most influential assumption and explain what new observation would change the choice.
