Choose sufficient verification evidence

Define the evidence that will establish a stated requirement for a named configuration. Select inspection for a visible property, analysis for behavior derived from a justified model, demonstration for an observable task, or test for a controlled measurement. Complex claims often need more than one method.

1. Define the observable, units, acceptance rule and instrument before sampling. Capture the specimen or build, environment, input population and expected disturbances. Identify shared causes that can bias nominally independent trials.
2. Set a rule for invalid runs and missing data. Preserve raw observations, the calculation that produced the result and the version of the decision rule. Agree who can interpret a borderline result.
3. Challenge the coverage of the method. Calibrate models against measurements. Where measurement uncertainty overlaps a limit, follow a pre-agreed decision rule or seek a more discriminating observation.

Example. A response-time ceiling is 100 ms. A conservative rule requires measured time plus a bounded 3 ms measurement allowance to fit within the limit. A reading of 98 ms leaves this claim unresolved. Record the observation, investigate measurement error, and plan a new run or improve the instrument.

Review record. A reviewer unfamiliar with the trial can reconstruct the verdict, its scope and each assumption that would invalidate reuse of the evidence.
