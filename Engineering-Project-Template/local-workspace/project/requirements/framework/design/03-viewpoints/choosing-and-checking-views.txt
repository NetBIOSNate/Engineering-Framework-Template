Select viewpoints from decisions

A viewpoint states what a reader needs to see, how the representation is constructed and the questions it should answer. A view applies those conventions to one system. Put the stakeholder and decision at the top of the view before choosing a drawing style.

Stakeholder and decision       View to prepare                   Consistency check
Sponsor sets the boundary      Context and outcome measures      Every external dependency has an accountable party.
Operator takes control         Modes and transitions             Each manual override names who can exercise it.
Integrator connects parts      Interface and allocation table    Two sides agree on units, custody and failure semantics.
Maintainer isolates a fault    Physical or deployment view       Diagnostic path exists while the failed element is unavailable.
Reviewer accepts a release     Claim and evidence map            Each claim names the tested revision and operating envelope.

Trace a single event across views. Suppose an operator disables a conveyor section. The mode table assigns the operator authority, the physical view identifies a local station, and the interface record describes the signal to the upstream section. If the sequence assumes the upstream section sees confirmation while disconnected, the inconsistency creates a requirement or a design issue. Assign it to one owner and record the affected views.

Select just enough detail to answer the open question. A control-room boundary drawing may stop at a cabinet. A maintainer replacing a board may need connector keying, isolation points and access clearance. Record why extra detail is worth maintaining and the change that requires its review.

Exit when another reviewer can reconstruct an ordinary and a degraded event using the views, identify the owner at each boundary, and explain how a changed requirement would alter a design choice.
